# CDS-OPS-003

## Emergency Escalation Procedure

**Document ID:** CDS-OPS-003  
**Revision:** Beginning 1e01  
**Status:** Approved  
**Master language:** English  
**Document series:** Operational Procedures  
**Responsible authority:** Cat Distribution System (CDS)

---

### 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the emergency escalation principles and actions applicable to situations involving a cat or a human in distress where immediate intervention may be necessary.

It provides a common escalation framework supporting CDS-OPS-001, *Response to a Cat in Distress*, and CDS-OPS-002, *Response to a Human in Distress*.

The purpose is to ensure that urgent situations receive appropriate attention and that real-world assistance is sought without delay.

### 2. Fundamental Principle

**An emergency takes precedence over all administrative procedures.**

Where a cat or human appears to face an immediate or potentially imminent threat to life, health or safety, appropriate real-world emergency assistance MUST be sought WITHOUT WAITING for administrative processing, assessment completion or authorisation from the System.

No administrative requirement may be interpreted as a reason to postpone emergency intervention.

### 3. Scope

This procedure applies to emergency situations involving:

- A cat experiencing a potentially life-threatening injury or condition.
- A cat exposed to immediate danger or an environment presenting a serious risk.
- A human experiencing a medical emergency or immediate threat to personal safety.
- A human whose circumstances indicate an imminent risk of serious harm.
- Any other situation in which delay may significantly worsen the outcome for a cat or human.

The procedure applies regardless of whether the affected individual or cat has previously been known to the System.

### 4. Emergency Escalation Procedure

#### 4.1 Recognition

An emergency may be identified through direct observation, information supplied by an individual, an existing case record or another available source.

Complete information or independent verification is not a prerequisite for initiating emergency assistance where the circumstances reasonably indicate an immediate risk.

#### 4.2 Immediate Action

Appropriate real-world emergency assistance MUST be contacted as soon as an emergency is identified.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

- Emergency medical services.
- Veterinary emergency services.
- Fire and rescue services.
- Police or other relevant public authorities.
- Animal rescue or welfare organisations.
- Other qualified emergency responders.

The choice of assistance should reflect the nature of the emergency and the location of the affected cat or human.

Where the appropriate service is uncertain, the available local emergency service should be contacted for guidance.

#### 4.3 Escalation Path

The following sequence establishes the general escalation path:

1. **Identify the emergency.** Recognise the apparent immediate risk to a cat or human.
2. **Initiate real-world assistance.** Contact the appropriate emergency service or qualified responder without delay.
3. **Provide available information.** Communicate the known circumstances, location and nature of the emergency.
4. **Follow professional instructions.** Cooperate with emergency responders and follow their guidance.
5. **Establish continuity.** Where possible, ensure that relevant individuals are aware of the situation and that assistance remains available until the immediate circumstances are addressed.
6. **Record the outcome.** Document the actions taken and any subsequent information when doing so no longer interferes with emergency response.

If the first contact cannot provide assistance, an alternative appropriate service MUST be sought. An unsuccessful initial contact does not constitute grounds for terminating escalation.

#### 4.4 Coordination

Where appropriate, relevant individuals and services may be informed of the emergency and the assistance being arranged.

Coordination must not interfere with the work of qualified responders or create additional delays.

The immediate needs of the affected cat or human remain the primary consideration.

#### 4.5 Unavailable or Incomplete Information

An absence of information, uncertainty regarding circumstances or inability to establish the full nature of an emergency must not prevent appropriate assistance from being sought.

Available information should be communicated accurately. Unknown details must not be presented as established facts.

### 5. Documentation

Emergency documentation is secondary to immediate intervention.

Where circumstances permit, the following information may be recorded:

- Identification of the affected cat or human, where available.
- Location and circumstances of the emergency.
- Time of identification, using applicable documentation conventions.
- Emergency services or responders contacted.
- Actions taken and instructions received.
- Subsequent developments and known outcomes.

Records may be completed or supplemented after the immediate emergency has been addressed.

### 6. Follow-up and Closure

Following the initial intervention, available information should be reviewed to determine whether further assistance, coordination or administrative action is required.

An emergency may be considered administratively concluded when the available information indicates that the immediate emergency has been addressed and no further escalation action is presently required.

Administrative closure does not constitute confirmation of full recovery or guarantee that further assistance will not be necessary.

Where new information indicates a renewed or continuing emergency, the escalation procedure MUST be initiated again.

### 7. Administrative Limitations

This procedure does not establish medical or veterinary diagnostic criteria, prescribe treatment, replace emergency services or assign professional responsibilities to CDS Administrators.

All real-world emergency intervention remains the responsibility of appropriate qualified professionals and relevant authorities.

CDS administrative coordination is supplementary and must never obstruct, delay or replace such intervention.

### 8. Final Provision

The absence of a complete record does not prevent emergency escalation.

The absence of an identified allocation case does not prevent emergency escalation.

The absence of confirmation does not justify ignoring an apparent emergency.

**When immediate assistance is required, action takes precedence over administration.**
