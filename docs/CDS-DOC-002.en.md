# CDS-DOC-002

## Terminology and Definitions

**Document ID:** CDS-DOC-002  
**Revision:** 0.1  
**Status:** Approved  
**Master language:** English  
**Subject:** Terminology used in the Cat Distribution System (CDS) and CATIS documentation

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a common terminology framework for documentation relating to the Cat Distribution System (CDS) and its technological platform, CATIS.

Its purpose is to ensure consistent use of names, concepts, and definitions across all official-language editions and related publications.

This document defines terminology only. It does not establish new operational procedures, technical capabilities, or internal mechanisms of the System.

## 2. Core Terms

### 2.1. Cat Distribution System (CDS)

The independent System that determines the patterns governing the appearance of cats in people's lives and coordinates their allocation processes, taking into account individual circumstances and suitable conditions.

CDS is the international identifier of the System.

### 2.2. The System

A shortened reference to the Cat Distribution System (CDS).

The term refers to CDS itself and must not be used to imply that CDS is a conventional institution, organisation, agency, or administrative centre.

### 2.3. CATIS

**Cat Allocation & Tracking Information System (CATIS)** is the technological platform supporting the processes of the Cat Distribution System.

CATIS and CDS are distinct entities. CATIS is not an alternative name for CDS.

### 2.4. System Administrators

The designated administrators of the System.

The purpose, capabilities, and operating principles of CATIS are accessible to System Administrators. This document does not define their organisational structure, individual responsibilities, permissions, or access procedures.

### 2.5. Cat Allocation

The process of coordinating the appearance of a cat in a person's life with consideration for individual circumstances and suitable conditions.

This term describes the general subject of the System's activities and does not establish a particular allocation procedure.

### 2.6. Candidate

A person who may become a recipient of a cat through processes associated with the System.

The use of this term does not imply that a formal application, registration, or selection procedure necessarily exists.

## 3. Documentation Terms

### 3.1. Master Language

The language that serves as the controlling reference for international documentation.

English is the master language of CDS and CATIS documentation.

### 3.2. Official Language Edition

A language-specific edition of an official document.

The six official languages of CDS documentation are English, French, Spanish, Russian, Chinese, and Arabic.

### 3.3. Parallel Edition

An official-language edition corresponding to the same document identifier and revision as the master-language document.

Parallel editions should preserve the meaning, scope, and status of the master document.

### 3.4. Confirmed Feature

A capability, characteristic, or requirement that has been formally established in the relevant approved documentation.

A statement in a proposal or preliminary document does not, by itself, constitute confirmation.

### 3.5. Proposal

A suggested concept, capability, requirement, or change that has not yet been formally approved.

Proposals must not be presented as established characteristics of CDS or CATIS.

## 4. Terminological Distinctions

The following distinctions must be maintained throughout CDS and CATIS documentation:

1. **CDS and CATIS:** the System and its supporting technological platform are distinct.
2. **The System and an organisation:** CDS must not be described as a conventional institution, agency, organisation, or centre.
3. **Cat allocation and a specific procedure:** the general concept of allocation does not establish any particular operational procedure.
4. **Confirmed features and proposals:** proposed capabilities must not be represented as implemented or approved.
5. **Observed outcomes and internal mechanisms:** descriptions of observable events and results must not be used to disclose or infer the undisclosed internal mechanisms of CDS.

## 5. Language and Translation Rules

1. English is the controlling language for international documentation.
2. Official editions in French, Spanish, Russian, Chinese, and Arabic are maintained as parallel versions.
3. The identifier CDS must remain consistent across all language editions.
4. The name CATIS and its full English expansion must remain unchanged.
5. Translations must preserve the conceptual distinction between CDS and CATIS.
6. Where a term has no direct equivalent in a target language, the translation must preserve its established meaning rather than introduce a new concept.

## 6. Scope and Limitations

This document establishes a common terminology framework but does not constitute a complete glossary of every term that may appear in future CDS or CATIS documentation.

Additional terms may be introduced through subsequent approved documents.

Any new definition must remain consistent with the established nature of CDS, the distinction between CDS and CATIS, and the documentation principles defined in the relevant approved documents.
