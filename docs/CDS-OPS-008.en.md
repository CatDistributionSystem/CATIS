# CDS-OPS-008

## Recording Observed System Manifestations

**Document status:** Approved  
**Document series:** Operational Procedures  
**Responsible authority:** Cat Distribution System (CDS)  
**Master language:** English  
**Revision:** 7e42.12

---

### 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the principles and administrative requirements for recording observed manifestations of the Cat Distribution System (CDS).

Its purpose is to ensure that relevant events, circumstances and outcomes are documented consistently, preserving the distinction between direct observation, reported information and subsequent interpretation.

The procedure provides a framework for maintaining records of System manifestations without requiring their underlying causes, mechanisms or processes to be explained.

### 2. Scope

This procedure applies to observations and reports concerning circumstances potentially associated with the System, including:

- The appearance of cats in people's lives.
- Unexpected encounters or developments involving cats and humans.
- Circumstances preceding or following an apparent allocation.
- Recurring or unusual patterns observed across individual cases.
- Events that may be relevant to the understanding or administration of the System.
- Outcomes that warrant preservation for future reference.

This procedure concerns the recording and administrative handling of observations. It does not establish a requirement to investigate, classify or explain every unusual event.

### 3. General Principles

#### 3.1 Observability

A manifestation may be recorded where relevant circumstances have been directly observed or reported through an identifiable source.

The absence of a complete explanation does not prevent an observation from being recorded.

#### 3.2 Separation of Observation and Interpretation

Records shall distinguish between what was observed, what was reported and what was subsequently inferred.

An interpretation shall not be presented as a confirmed observation.

#### 3.3 Preservation of Original Information

Where practicable, the original description of an event should be preserved.

Subsequent clarification or interpretation should not silently replace the initial record.

#### 3.4 No Requirement for Explanation

The recording of a manifestation does not require an explanation of how or why it occurred.

The System's underlying principles and mechanisms are not subject to disclosure through this procedure.

#### 3.5 Relevance

Not every unusual event requires formal documentation.

The decision to record an observation should reflect its potential relevance to individual cases, administrative continuity or the wider understanding of observable System manifestations.

### 4. Identification of Manifestations

A potential System manifestation may be identified through:

- Direct observation by an individual.
- Information supplied by a person involved in the circumstances.
- Reports from third parties.
- Information arising during the handling of an existing allocation case.
- Comparison with previously recorded observations.
- Other circumstances brought to the attention of the System.

The source of the information should be recorded where known.

The identification of a potential manifestation does not, by itself, establish its classification or significance.

### 5. Recording Requirements

Where an observation is selected for formal recording, the record should include, where available:

- A unique observation reference.
- A concise description of the observed or reported event.
- The circumstances in which it occurred.
- The relevant cat or human, where identifiable.
- The source of the information.
- The distinction between direct observation and reported information.
- Supporting material, where available and appropriate.
- Any related allocation case or existing record.
- Subsequent developments or clarifications.

Information that is unavailable should remain unspecified.

Records should not introduce details solely to create the appearance of completeness.

### 6. Classification of Information

For consistency, recorded information should be distinguished according to its evidential status.

**6.1 Directly Observed**

Information based on an event or circumstance personally observed by the reporting individual.

**6.2 Reported**

Information supplied by an individual concerning an event or circumstance they describe but which has not been independently observed by the recording Administrator.

**6.3 Corroborated**

Information supported by additional independent observations, records or sources.

**6.4 Inferred**

An interpretation or conclusion derived from available information rather than directly established by observation.

**6.5 Unconfirmed**

Information for which the available material is insufficient to establish its accuracy or status.

These classifications describe the status of information, not the significance or validity of the underlying event.

### 7. Review and Correlation

Recorded manifestations may be reviewed individually or in relation to other observations and allocation cases.

Such review may identify:

- Similarities between reported circumstances.
- Repeated or recurring patterns.
- Relevant relationships between separate events.
- Additional information that may clarify an existing record.
- Circumstances warranting further administrative attention.

A similarity between observations does not, by itself, establish a common cause.

Correlations should be recorded as observations or assessments and must not be presented as proof of an underlying mechanism.

### 8. Significance and Administrative Assessment

An Administrator may assign an administrative assessment to an observation where its relevance warrants further attention.

Such assessments may concern:

- Relevance to an existing allocation case.
- Potential significance for future case handling.
- Relevance to other recorded manifestations.
- Need for clarification or additional information.
- Need for preservation as a reference record.

The absence of an assigned significance does not require deletion or disregard of an otherwise relevant observation.

The System does not require every recorded manifestation to produce an administrative action.

### 9. Record Maintenance

Observation records should be maintained in accordance with applicable CATIS documentation conventions.

The record should preserve:

- The original observation or report.
- Its source and evidential classification.
- Subsequent clarifications.
- Administrative assessments.
- Links to related records, where applicable.
- Any changes to the recorded status.

Where information is later found to be inaccurate or incomplete, the record should be corrected or supplemented without obscuring its history.

### 10. Relationship with Other Operational Procedures

This procedure operates alongside the other CDS operational procedures.

Where an observation concerns an individual allocation case, CDS-OPS-007 shall apply as appropriate.

Where an observation identifies distress or an emergency, the applicable response and escalation procedures shall take precedence.

Where assistance has been provided and its outcome requires verification, CDS-OPS-004 shall apply.

Recording an observation shall never delay necessary real-world assistance.

### 11. Administrative Limitations

This procedure does not establish a scientific method for determining the causes of System manifestations.

It does not authorise Administrators to present unverified interpretations as established facts or to disclose restricted System information through observation records.

The purpose of documentation is to preserve relevant information and support administrative continuity.

### 12. Final Provision

A manifestation may be observed without being understood.

An observation may be recorded without being explained.

A pattern may be recognised without its cause being established.

**The absence of an explanation does not diminish the value of an observation.**
