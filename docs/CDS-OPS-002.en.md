# CDS-OPS-002

## Response to a Human in Distress

**Document ID:** CDS-OPS-002  
**Revision:** Beginning 1e01  
**Status:** Approved  
**Master language:** English  
**Document series:** Operational Procedures  
**Responsible authority:** Cat Distribution System (CDS)

---

### 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the principles and administrative steps governing the System's response to situations in which a human is observed or reported to be in distress.

The procedure is intended to ensure that such situations receive appropriate attention, that relevant circumstances are recorded, and that suitable assistance is not delayed by administrative requirements.

### 2. Fundamental Principle

**A human in distress is sufficient reason for the System to act.**

The existence of an allocation case, registration record, identified candidate or previous contact with the System is not a prerequisite for initiating a response.

The circumstances of the individual take precedence over the completeness of their administrative record.

### 3. Scope

This procedure applies to situations involving, but not limited to:

- A person experiencing emotional or psychological distress.
- A person experiencing social isolation or significant personal difficulties.
- A person facing circumstances that adversely affect their well-being.
- A person experiencing loss, disruption or a significant change in their living circumstances.
- A person whose situation indicates a need for support or assistance.
- Any other situation in which an individual's well-being may be at risk.

The procedure applies regardless of whether the individual has previously been known to the System or has participated in an allocation process.

### 4. Response Procedure

#### 4.1 Identification

A distress situation may become known through direct observation, information supplied by an individual, an existing case record or another observable manifestation.

The absence of complete or independently verified information does not, by itself, justify disregarding a report.

#### 4.2 Initial Assessment

The available information should be reviewed to establish:

- The individual's apparent circumstances.
- The nature of the reported or observed distress.
- Whether immediate assistance may be required.
- Whether suitable assistance is already available.
- Whether further information is necessary.

The assessment should reflect the available evidence and must not present assumptions as established facts.

#### 4.3 Immediate Assistance

Where an individual appears to require urgent assistance, appropriate real-world assistance MUST be sought WITHOUT WAITING for administrative processing.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include emergency services, healthcare professionals, social support services, relevant authorities or individuals capable of providing suitable immediate assistance.

CDS administrative procedures do not replace professional medical assessment, emergency intervention or the responsibilities of qualified professionals.

**Administrative completeness must never take precedence over the immediate well-being of the individual.**

#### 4.4 Coordination

Where appropriate, the System may coordinate the available information and the involvement of relevant individuals or services.

The response should take account of the individual's circumstances, expressed needs and the suitability of the assistance available.

No particular outcome should be presumed solely on the basis of an initial assessment.

#### 4.5 Follow-up

Where information remains available, subsequent developments should be recorded.

These may include:

- Changes in the individual's circumstances.
- Assistance provided or arranged.
- Further observations or information.
- Confirmation of the individual's current situation.
- Any additional action considered necessary.

### 5. Documentation

A record of the situation may be created in accordance with applicable CATIS documentation conventions.

Where information is available, the record should distinguish between:

- Direct observations.
- Information reported by the individual or third parties.
- Assessment and administrative conclusions.
- Actions taken.
- Subsequent developments.

Missing information should not be replaced with assumptions.

An existing allocation record is not required to document a distress situation.

### 6. Case Status and Closure

A distress situation may be considered administratively concluded when the available information indicates that the immediate circumstances have been addressed or that no further administrative action is presently required.

Administrative closure does not constitute a guarantee of the individual's continued well-being.

Where circumstances change or new information becomes available, the situation may receive further attention.

### 7. Administrative Limitations

This procedure does not establish medical or psychological diagnostic criteria, prescribe treatment or replace the responsibilities of qualified professionals and relevant authorities.

It governs the System's administrative attention, coordination and documentation of distress situations.

### 8. Final Provision

The absence of a complete record does not negate an observed circumstance.

The absence of an identified allocation case does not prevent a response.

**The well-being of the individual remains the primary consideration throughout the procedure.**
