# CDS-PROC-001

## Candidate Assessment Procedure

**Document ID:** CDS-PROC-001  
**Revision:** 7e65.4  
**Status:** Approved  
**Master language:** English  
**Subject:** General principles for the assessment of candidates for cat allocation

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the general principles for assessing individuals who may become recipients of a cat through processes associated with the Cat Distribution System (CDS).

Its purpose is to provide a consistent framework for considering individual circumstances, the suitability of conditions, and the overall context surrounding a potential allocation.

This document does not explain the internal mechanisms of CDS or establish how the System reaches its decisions.

## 2. Scope

The procedure applies to the general concept of candidate assessment within the CDS framework.

Assessment may take into consideration circumstances relating to:

- the candidate;
- the candidate's living environment;
- existing household arrangements;
- the presence of other animals;
- the candidate's ability to provide appropriate care;
- other circumstances relevant to a suitable allocation.

The list is indicative and must not be interpreted as an exhaustive set of requirements.

## 3. Fundamental Assessment Principles

Candidate assessment is guided by the following principles:

### 3.1. Individual Consideration

Each candidate is considered in the context of their individual circumstances.

General assumptions must not replace consideration of the specific situation.

### 3.2. Suitability of Conditions

The circumstances surrounding a potential allocation should be considered with regard to the welfare, safety, and needs of the cat.

### 3.3. Comprehensive Context

Relevant circumstances must be considered together rather than treated as isolated factors.

An individual circumstance may have a different significance depending on the broader context.

### 3.4. Non-Deterministic Assessment

No single characteristic or observation should automatically be treated as a definitive indication of suitability or unsuitability.

### 3.5. Confidentiality

Information concerning candidates and individual allocation circumstances must be treated with appropriate discretion.

Public documentation must not disclose personal information or details that could compromise the privacy of individuals involved.

## 4. Areas of Consideration

The following areas may be relevant to candidate assessment.

### 4.1. Living Environment

Consideration may include the general suitability and safety of the environment in which the cat may live.

### 4.2. Household Circumstances

The composition of the household, existing responsibilities, and the presence of other animals may be relevant.

### 4.3. Readiness for Cat Ownership

The candidate's understanding of the responsibilities associated with caring for a cat may be considered.

This includes the ability and willingness to provide appropriate care over time.

### 4.4. Individual Behaviour and Circumstances

Relevant behavioural patterns, personal circumstances, and interactions with animals may form part of the broader assessment.

No single observation should be interpreted in isolation or treated as a definitive psychological or behavioural diagnosis.

### 4.5. Compatibility

The circumstances of the candidate and the characteristics and needs of the cat may be considered together.

The objective is not to establish an abstract ranking of candidates, but to recognise conditions that may support a suitable allocation.

## 5. Assessment Outcomes

The result of an assessment may be described using the following general categories:

1. **Suitable circumstances observed.** The available information indicates conditions that may support a suitable allocation.
2. **Additional circumstances to be considered.** Relevant information is incomplete or further consideration may be appropriate.
3. **Unsuitable circumstances observed.** The available information indicates circumstances that may not support a suitable allocation at the present time.
4. **No definitive conclusion.** The available information does not support a reliable assessment.

These categories describe possible assessment findings. They do not constitute a disclosure of the System's internal decision-making mechanisms or guarantee a particular outcome.

## 6. Documentation

Where assessment findings are documented, records should distinguish between:

- directly observed circumstances;
- information provided by the candidate or other sources;
- professional or practical observations;
- assumptions or interpretations;
- the resulting assessment summary.

Unverified information must not be presented as established fact.

Records should be limited to information relevant to the assessment and handled with appropriate confidentiality.

## 7. Relationship to Other Documentation

This procedure should be read in conjunction with:

- **CDS-DOC-001 — Foundational Description**, which establishes the fundamental nature and principles of CDS;
- **CDS-DOC-002 — Terminology and Definitions**, which establishes the terminology used in CDS documentation;
- **CATIS-TECH-001 — CATIS Technical Platform Overview**, which defines the position and documentation scope of the technological platform supporting CDS.

Detailed methodological approaches to behavioural analysis may be established in separate documentation.

## 8. Limitations

This document describes general assessment principles only.

It does not establish:

- a formal application or registration process;
- a numerical scoring system or candidate ranking;
- mandatory eligibility thresholds;
- a fixed sequence of assessment stages;
- automated decision-making capabilities;
- specific CATIS functionality for candidate assessment.

Such matters must not be considered established features of CDS or CATIS unless separately defined in approved documentation.

The internal mechanisms through which the System operates remain undisclosed.
