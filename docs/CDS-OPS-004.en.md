# CDS-OPS-004

## Monitoring and Follow-up of Assistance Outcomes

**Document ID:** CDS-OPS-004  
**Revision:** 7e35.12  
**Status:** Approved  
**Master language:** English  
**Document series:** Operational Procedures  
**Responsible authority:** Cat Distribution System (CDS)

---

### 1. Purpose

This procedure establishes the principles and administrative steps governing the monitoring and follow-up of assistance provided in response to situations involving cats or humans in distress.

Its purpose is to ensure that assistance is not merely recorded as an isolated intervention, but that its subsequent outcome receives appropriate attention.

The procedure establishes a consistent approach to determining whether the circumstances that prompted the original response have improved, been resolved or continue to require attention.

### 2. Fundamental Principle

**The provision of assistance does not, in itself, constitute confirmation that the circumstances have been resolved.**

The System should distinguish between assistance arranged, assistance actually provided and the outcome subsequently established.

The completion of an intervention does not automatically constitute the resolution of the situation.

### 3. Scope

This procedure applies to assistance provided to cats or humans in circumstances addressed under:

- CDS-OPS-001 — Response to a Cat in Distress.
- CDS-OPS-002 — Response to a Human in Distress.
- CDS-OPS-003 — Emergency Escalation Procedure.

It applies to assistance coordinated, arranged, reported or otherwise recorded by the System, regardless of whether the original situation was classified as an emergency.

### 4. Recording Assistance Provided

Where information is available, the record should identify:

- The cat or human concerned, where known.
- The circumstances that prompted the original response.
- The nature of the assistance provided or arranged.
- The individuals, organisations or services involved, where known.
- The date and circumstances of the intervention, using applicable documentation conventions.
- The expected or intended outcome, where established.

The record should distinguish between assistance that was proposed, arranged, attempted and confirmed as provided.

Information that has not been independently established should be identified accordingly.

### 5. Follow-up Procedure

#### 5.1 Initiation

Follow-up should be initiated when an intervention has been reported or confirmed as provided and sufficient information is available to assess subsequent developments.

The timing and extent of follow-up should reflect the nature of the original circumstances, the assistance provided and any known continuing needs.

#### 5.2 Obtaining Information

Relevant information may be obtained through:

- Direct observation.
- Information supplied by the affected individual.
- Information supplied by persons involved in providing assistance.
- Updates from relevant professionals or organisations.
- Existing case records and subsequent observations.

The absence of new information should not be interpreted as confirmation that the situation has been resolved.

#### 5.3 Review of Circumstances

Available information should be reviewed to establish:

- Whether the intended assistance was provided.
- Whether the original circumstances have changed.
- Whether the immediate needs have been addressed.
- Whether further assistance may be required.
- Whether new or additional concerns have arisen.

The review should reflect the available evidence and must not present assumptions as established facts.

### 6. Outcome Assessment

For consistent documentation, the following outcome categories may be used:

**6.1 Resolved**

Available information confirms that the circumstances requiring assistance have been addressed and no further assistance is presently indicated.

**6.2 Improved**

The circumstances have improved, but some needs or concerns remain.

**6.3 Unchanged**

The circumstances remain substantially as they were when the original situation was identified.

**6.4 Deteriorated**

Available information indicates that the circumstances have worsened or additional concerns have arisen.

**6.5 Unknown or Unconfirmed**

Insufficient information is available to establish the outcome.

An outcome category should reflect the information available at the time of assessment. It may be revised when further information becomes available.

### 7. Further Action

Where follow-up indicates that additional assistance may be necessary, the situation should receive further attention in accordance with the applicable operational procedures.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include:

- Arranging additional assistance.
- Reinitiating the response procedure under CDS-OPS-001 or CDS-OPS-002.
- Initiating emergency escalation under CDS-OPS-003.
- Requesting further information from relevant individuals or services.
- Establishing an additional follow-up review.

Where an immediate or potentially imminent threat to life, health or safety is identified, emergency assistance MUST be sought WITHOUT WAITING for follow-up documentation or administrative review.

### 8. Documentation

All relevant follow-up activities and available outcomes should be recorded in accordance with applicable CATIS documentation conventions.

Records should distinguish between:

- Assistance proposed or arranged.
- Assistance confirmed as provided.
- Information obtained during follow-up.
- Observed or reported changes in circumstances.
- Outcome assessment.
- Further actions taken or considered.

Where the outcome remains unknown or unconfirmed, this status should be retained rather than replaced with an assumed resolution.

### 9. Administrative Closure

A case may be considered administratively concluded when the available information indicates that:

- The assistance provided has been appropriately recorded.
- The outcome has been assessed to the extent reasonably possible.
- No further administrative action is presently required.

Administrative closure does not constitute a guarantee of permanent resolution or continued well-being.

Where new information becomes available or circumstances change, the case may receive further attention.

### 10. Administrative Limitations

This procedure does not establish medical, veterinary or psychological diagnostic criteria, prescribe treatment or replace the responsibilities of qualified professionals and relevant authorities.

Outcome assessments are administrative records based on available information and should not be interpreted as professional certification of recovery or resolution.

### 11. Final Provision

Assistance provided must be distinguished from assistance confirmed.

An intervention completed must be distinguished from a situation resolved.

An unknown outcome must remain unknown until sufficient information becomes available.

**The System's responsibility to follow a situation does not end merely because an intervention has taken place.**
